# Actions: heal, buff, debuff, shouts, transformations, dual casting, attack, powers and food

What the engine lets a follower do on request, from research done
2026-09-04 (sources at the end). The existing actions are potions, a cast
through a UseMagic package, and the equip pins. This is about what the
next ones can be built on, and what they cannot.

## 1. Heal, buff and debuff on someone else

**Vanilla combat AI never casts a beneficial spell on anyone but itself.**
The caster classes in its list are offensive, restore-self, ward, armour,
summon, bound weapon, cloak, invisibility, light, disarm, paralyse,
reanimate, stagger, target-effect and scripted; `Restore` is self only.
Follower-healer mods (Be With Healer, MSFF, the 3DNPC healers) all do it
the way we already cast: a package that overrides combat with the spell
and a target input.

So **heal / buff an ally is our CastSpell with a target**, and the pieces
are in place: the package's Target input takes a single reference, the
lease repoints it per cast, and the step already carries a target actor.
What is missing is (a) Execute honouring the step's target for a targeted
spell rather than always the follower's combat target, and (b) a way to
choose the target in the editor. Both are small. Aimed delivery (Healing
Hands, a stream) at a moving ally is unverified; Heal Other and Grand
Healing are fire-and-forget or area and safer to start with. Healing Hands
on a follower can aggro them on some records; Heal Other does not.

**Debuffs do not exist as castable spells in vanilla.** Weakness to
Fire/Frost/Shock/Magic/Poison are alchemy effects, poisons, abilities and
one shout (Marked for Death); no spell record references them. Apocalypse,
Odin and Mysticism add castable ones. So "debuff enemy" is: poisons on the
weapon (a Use Poison action, which NPCsUsePotions already does for NPCs),
Marked for Death if the follower can shout, or a spell mod. Buffing an
ally's armour or resistance is the same story: vanilla's Stoneflesh line
and the resist spells are self-only; targeted versions need a mod's
records or ones of our own in the ESP.

## 2. Shouts

**Any NPC with the shout in their spell list can shout**; the voice type
only decides whether the words are audible. Ulfric and the Greybeards are
ordinary actors with a TESShout listed; Shouty People distributes seven
shouts through SPID and they are used. The combat AI scores a shout entry
by `shoutScoreMult` and equips it when the voice recovery time is below a
game setting.

**The cooldown is per actor**, in the high process data: `voiceRecoveryTime`, read by `Actor::GetVoiceRecoveryTime()`, is the countdown itself, the seconds left, not a recovery with the time since kept beside it. Watched 2026-09-15: a countdown of 220.48 s read 220.48 in `voiceRecoveryTime` and 0 in `voiceTimeElapsed`, which is something else, and `currentShout` was empty once the shout had fired; so once a shout is out the game keeps neither which shout set the countdown nor the recovery it set, and the Magic pages' Cooldown is the countdown alone. Each word has its own recovery time on the shout record, scaled by the ShoutRecoveryMult actor value (the word rows write it out). CommonLib has no setter; the field can be written.

**On demand:** the engine has a `Shout` package procedure (Shout, Target,
HoldWhenBlocked), used by the Greybeard training; it is the shout twin of
UseMagic and splices into the alias the same way. `EquipShout` alone only
selects the power; the package or the AI fires it. Untested whether the
procedure honours the recovery time.

So a **Shout action** is: a `Shout` package beside each follower's UseMagic
one, a lease per cast, a snapshot field for the recovery time so the rule
can report "cooling down" rather than fire into it, and the follower must
know the shout (a `Learn shout` action, or the player teaches it).

**The words are dialogue, and most followers cannot say them (found 2026-09-09).** A shout's spoken words are lines under the Voice Powers quest (`013E06`): topics `Shout01a`, `01b`, `02`, `03` (`013E01..013DFE`, subtypes VoicePowerStartShort/Long), one line per shout, each conditioned on `GetEquippedShout` and on `GetIsVoiceType` against a list. Two lists carry recordings: `VoicesPlayer` (`068ACA`) -- the ten voice types a player can have, Male and Female EvenToned, Orc, Khajiit, Argonian, ElfHaughty -- and `VoicePowerVoicesList` (`10D29D`), those plus Arngeir, Gormlaith, Felldir, Hakon and the draugr creature voice. Dragons are apart: their words are sound descriptors on the shout effects (`VOCShoutDragon*`, with Paarthurnax's and Alduin's own), not lines. A follower with any other voice type -- most named followers -- has no line and shouts in silence, or in whatever else the load order plays; a male follower's voice type is likelier to be on the list than a female's, which is how "female followers shout in a male voice" showed. So `RequestShout` lends the record the EvenToned voice type of the follower's sex for the lease when their own is on neither list (`LendShoutVoice`), and gives it back on release, on reset and on abandon; the condition reads the record's voice type at the shout. The cost: a bark in the same two seconds comes out in the lent voice. A follower whose base record takes its traits from a template is not covered, since the voice is read through the template. Not yet heard in play.

## 3. Transformations

Werewolf and Vampire Lord are **powers whose effect scripts support
NPCs**: the effect always casts the transform-visual spell, which on a
non-player does `SetRace(WerewolfRace)` / `SetRace(DLC1VampireLordRace)`
after the animation event. Vanilla uses it on Sinding, Aela and Harkon.

What an NPC does **not** get is everything the player quest handles:
duration, the shift back, feeding, re-equipping gear afterwards. That is
ours to do: `Actor::SwitchRace` back to the original race on a rule or a
timer, and put the outfit back on (the pin book can hold it). Vampire Lord
combat on an NPC is the known problem, magic mode "extremely buggy with
NPCs"; werewolf mods (Beastblood, The Beast Within, Growl) are all script
and all cope. A **Transform action** is therefore: cast the power at the
follower through the instant caster or a UseMagic package, remember the
race, and a paired **Revert** with the outfit restore. Plausible; test
werewolf first.

## 4. Dual casting

**NPCs can dual-cast; vanilla mage bosses do.** The combat behaviour tree
has a PrepareDualCast node, and 139 vanilla NPC records carry the school
dual-casting perks. The gate is the perk entry point CanDualCastSpell,
which the school's Dual Casting perk sets, so a follower needs that perk
(`Actor::AddPerk`). No combat-style flag is involved.

Through **our package**, the UseMagic procedure has a DualCast boolean input; **built 2026-09-09 as the Dual Cast menu**, after Cast. A CastSpell action carries a `dual` flag (`"dual": true` on the wire). Which spells the menu offers and the evaluator allows is `CanDualCast` (`src/game/Sensors.cpp`), what one costs is `DualCastCost` beside it, and the perk switch is in `docs/PROFILES.md`. On request the slot's DualCast input is set, and cleared again for a one-handed cast since the record is shared (`SetPackageBool`, `src/game/Packages.cpp`). Not yet measured in play: whether the package honours the perk gate or bypasses it, and whether the follower's cast shows as dual. Serana's Ice Storm (2026-09-16, in `CanDualCast`'s comment) cannot tell: that spell is locked to the right hand, and Serana has no Dual Casting perk either. Direct API, unused: dual cast is state on the hand caster (`ActorMagicCaster::SetDualCasting`), not a cast argument. Watch for po3's Dual Casting Fix: scripted casts clear the dual state, and our faction-rank abilities might too.

**Scrolls (built 2026-09-09, not yet seen in play).** A Scroll action under Cast and Dual Cast, listing the scrolls carried by the scroll's own delivery: a Self one under Self, an aimed one under everyone else, as spells are. A scroll is a MagicItem cast from a hand like a spell, so it rides the UseMagic package with the scroll as the package's spell; the sensor lists carried scrolls in the snapshot's known list, since knowing and carrying are the one question, and no magicka is checked. Open: whether the engine spends the scroll on a package cast. The pool keeps the count from the request and, on the fire event, takes one off by hand if the count did not drop (`SpendScroll`), so a rule can never read the same scroll for free either way; the log says which happened.

## 5. What to build, in order

1. **Cast on a chosen target**: Execute takes the step's target; the
   Then cascade's first level names it (self, the ally or enemy the
   condition matched, the player, a named follower, the target, the
   attacker), as the If cascade's names the subject. Heal the player, heal the hurt ally, buff the one about
   to engage. Vanilla spells only.
2. **Use poison** on the weapon: done, the Apply cascade (below).
3. **Shout**: a Shout package per follower, the recovery time in the snapshot.
4. **Dual cast** as a flag on a cast action, needing the perk.
5. **Transform / Revert**, werewolf first.

## 6. Attack: focus aggression on one enemy

Research 2026-09-04. **The engine has no "attack" verb.** An actor in combat attacks whoever the combat controller's *target selector* has chosen; the behaviour tree and the inventory scoring then decide how (swing, shoot, cast). So the action is "focus on this enemy", and once the target changes the follower's own scoring picks the weapon or spell for it. Nothing here needs to branch by class.

**What exists, verified against the CommonLibSSE headers in this build tree:** `CombatController` has `targetHandle`, `previousTargetHandle`, a `targetSelectors` array and a `currentTargetSelector`; `Actor` mirrors the target as `currentCombatTarget`. Two selector classes are in the RTTI and vtable tables, `CombatTargetSelectorStandard` (the AI's own choice) and `CombatTargetSelectorFixed` (the engine's own "this one, regardless"). Neither has a mapped layout, so using the fixed one means reverse engineering. The `CombatGroup` holds one `targets` list for the whole group with a threat value and attacker count per target; followers and the player share a group.

**What the game files say (Skyrim.esm through houseCARL):** no game setting tunes target selection. The 96 `*Combat*` settings cover stealth points, regen, dialogue timers and radii; the only threat-related one is `fCombatThreatRatioUpdateTime` = 5 s. The INIs (game default, My Games, MO2 profiles) carry no target or selector settings either. So the standard selector's weights are hardcoded and can only be measured.

**Papyrus `StartCombat(target)` is not the tool.** The CK wiki says only "attempts to get this actor to initiate combat with the target". All 155 vanilla scripts that call it start a fight (Jorrvaskr brawl, Vilkas training, Cicero, the Silver Hand ambush); none retargets an actor already fighting, and that case is undocumented. A one-console-command test would settle what it does mid-combat.

**Bethesda's own "keep attacking this one" is a UseWeapon combat override.** Karliah and Brynjolf in Blindsighted run `TG08B*UseWeaponCombatOverride`: template `UseWeaponAlreadyHeld`, Target to Attack = an alias, Never End = true, `InterruptOverride = Combat`. The template's inputs are Weapon Type, Target to Attack, Always Hit, Do No Damage, Hold when Blocked, Never End, barrage counts and pauses, Max Time spent Attacking, Always Power Attack, Headtrack Target. The CK wiki adds: the actor attacks only "if the actor has a weapon of the specified Weapon type", and with an object list only the first target counts. So it is weapon-bound: a caster would need UseMagic instead, and the package drives the attacks rather than the combat style. Our eight `FT_CastSlot` records are the UseMagic twin (template `UseMagic`, flag IgnoreCombat, a GetIsReference condition) put at the front of the follower's own package stack for the lease (docs/MAGIC.md), so a UseWeapon pool would go the same way.

**Routes, in the order to try them:**

1. **Assert the target directly.** Each tick while a focus lease is held, write `targetHandle` and `currentCombatTarget` to the enemy and measure whether the standard selector snaps back before the next 500 ms tick. Twenty lines. If it holds, the whole AI stays in charge of everything but the target, which is the "bias, don't puppet" principle exactly.
2. **Hook the standard selector.** A vtable write like the score hook, returning our target while the lease lives. Cleanest, but the virtual's index and signature are unknown. Unverified.
3. **A UseWeapon package per follower**, placed like the cast packages, with UseMagic for casters. Heaviest, class-branching, and it puppets.

Availability: the target is alive, hostile and in the follower's combat group. Release on death, on combat end, or after a timeout, like a cast lease.

**Built 2026-09-04 as the `Attack` action (named Target until 2026-09-09), route 1.** `IF Ally: Attacked by Ranged THEN Attacker: Attack` is the shape. Core: the action is available only in combat, only when its target is one of the snapshot's enemies, and not when they are already the follower's current target, so a standing rule falls through instead of re-firing; its cooldown is 2 s keyed by the action alone, so two Attack rules cannot flick the follower between two enemies on consecutive ticks. Game: `Execute` writes the combat controller's `targetHandle`, `previousTargetHandle` and `cachedTarget`, and the actor's `currentCombatTarget`, and logs the old and new target. There is no separate instrumentation for the open question: if the standard selector lets the choice stand the rule reports "already fighting them" on the next tick, and if it snaps back the rule fires again after its cooldown, so the log answers it. Not yet measured in play. Ranged is a damage kind of its own: a physical hit whose event carries a projectile; a blow without one is Melee.

**Power Attack (built 2026-09-09; through a UseWeapon package since 2026-09-15, `docs/ATTACK.md`).** One swing. With the right hand's weapon it goes through the follower's UseWeapon record; otherwise by the animation event the race's attack data names for what is in the hands, sent with `NotifyAnimationGraph`. The hands are asked right first, then left, as a poison goes on: the right hand's blade or two-hander `attackPowerStartInPlace`, both blades at once `attackPowerStartDualWield`, else the left's blade `attackPowerStartInPlaceLeftHand` whatever the right holds, else the fists the right hand's event. No swing for a bow, a staff or a spell in the hand: those bash or cast. At an enemy who is not the follower's target, it points them there first, as Attack does; like Attack it is offered under an enemy and the attacker, not under Self, since it is done to them. The race record (Nord, `013746`) carries these events with their damage and stamina multipliers -- x2 and 1 for a one- or two-hand power attack, x1.5 and 0.5 for the dual-wield one -- and a chance per attack, which is how often the combat AI picks it on its own (0.2 for the directional power attacks, 0 for the sprint ones); DefaultRace lists eight events and no left-hand ones, so a creature follower needs its race checked. The stamina cost is the engine's own routine's (26429 on 1.6.1170, read 2026-09-15; the UseWeapon procedure asks it too): (the right hand's weapon's weight, 1 with no weapon there, x `fStaminaAttackWeaponMult` 1 + `fStaminaAttackWeaponBase` 20) x `fPowerAttackStaminaPenalty` 2, then the Mod Power Attack Stamina perk entry point with that weapon or with Unarmed (0001F4), then x the attack entry's own stamina multiplier, read from the follower's attack data by the event. A left-hand swing is priced by the right hand, as the engine prices it. UESP's formula, used unchecked until then, had no penalty and priced a power attack at half: a rule fired at 29 stamina for an Iron Sword the engine charges 58, and the UseWeapon procedure, which skips an attack the follower cannot pay for, made none. The evaluator refuses it out of a fight, with nothing that swings ("no weapon"), below the cost ("no stamina"), and at an enemy beyond the swing's reach ("too far"; a swing that lands on nothing is not charged stamina, observed in play, and is not made). The engine's melee range is `Actor::GetReach` (AE id 38538, read 2026-09-09): the right hand's weapon reach times `fCombatDistance` (141; a sword's 1.0 is 141 units, a dagger's 0.7 is 99, a greatsword's 1.3 is 183), or the race's unarmed reach with nothing in hand, times the actor's scale, and a further multiplier for the player in first person; a swing's victim is found with it (38608 and 47272 on 1.6.1170, read 2026-09-15): the actor's current target when in front and no further than the reach, else the nearest actor that is. The distance (47273) is centre to centre, flat when their heights differ by 48 or more and their bounds overlap in height, less both bodies' radii (47276: each actor's bound max Y times its scale, 16 for an empty box). The evaluator holds the reach against the same measure, taken on the game side for each enemy (`reachDistance`). Until 2026-09-15 it was a fixed 40-unit margin against the centre-to-centre distance, which refused a follower at a cave bear for ten seconds at a time. A bash's reach (`fCombatBashReach` at the actor's scale) is held against the same measure; that the engine measures a bash that way was not read. The UseWeapon procedure makes no range test of its own; one swing per firing, 1.5 s keyed by the action alone. Execute refuses it with the weapon sheathed, mid-swing, or with the graph turning the event away -- three results of its own since 2026-09-09, each named in the log at info. Open: whether the engine charges the stamina for an event sent this way; if it does not, the follower swings for free and the cost has to be taken by hand.

**Measured in play 2026-09-09: the blows land about a quarter of the time.** One session's log, eleven firings across the three: three performed (one power attack, two power bashes), eight refused. The AI's own attack state machine was the answer to the open question above -- it does NOT reliably let the event through mid-fight, because the follower's combat AI is driving the same graph and is usually already in a swing, a block or a recovery when our tick arrives.

Two things made that hard to see and are now fixed. All eight refusals printed **"every package slot is mid-cast"**, the text of `ActionResult::Busy`, which the blow path was reusing -- a message about a package pool no blow ever touches, and one word for three different problems; each now has its own result and its own sentence. And the comment here said the refusal cost no cooldown and the rule would try again on the next tick. It does cost one: the core stamps the action's cooldown in `Evaluate`, when the decision is made, before the game side ever dispatches it. So a refused blow waits the full 1.5 s (1.0 s for a plain bash) before the next attempt, at 500 ms ticks -- two ticks skipped for every attempt made.

That is the lever if a quarter is not enough: dispatching a blow is cheap and refusal is free, so the cooldown could be spent on the *result* rather than on the decision, letting a refused blow retry on the very next tick. That means the game side reporting back into the evaluation context, which nothing does today, so it is a design change and not a tweak. Not made yet, and the honest reason to wait is that a follower who power-attacks every 1.5 s whenever the graph allows may simply look frantic; the number to watch first is whether three in eleven is a problem in play or just what a follower's own attack rhythm looks like. `docs/ATTACK.md` is the case for a UseWeapon package instead of the events, after a Bash rule failed seven times in 34 s in Nordic Souls (2026-09-14), and since 2026-09-15 what was built: power attacks through the package, bashes from a raised block.

**Bash and Power Bash (built 2026-09-09; made from a raised block since 2026-09-15, `docs/ATTACK.md`).** The race data's other two blows: `bashStart`, flagged Bash Attack, and `bashPowerStart`, flagged Bash Attack and Power Attack, x1.5 damage and the hardest stagger of any entry. What blocks is what bashes, by the vanilla block rule: a shield or a torch in the left hand, else any weapon in the right hand with the left hand empty (a one-hander, a two-hander, a bow, a crossbow, a staff). A weapon alone in the left hand cannot block, so a left-hand staff does not bash; nor do two hands each holding something (a blade and a staff, a blade and a spell), the fists, or a spell hand ("no weapon"). The cost is the setting for the kind, `fStaminaBashBase` 35 and `fStaminaPowerBashBase` 55, times the entry's multiplier (1 for both); no perk entry point prices a bash, as the engine's own routine confirms (26429). The reach is `fCombatBashReach` (141) at the actor's scale, held against the engine's own measure as a swing's is (the reach paragraph above); that the engine measures a bash that way was not read. The player needs the Block perk Power Bash (058F67; Shield Wall and Block 30 to take it) to power bash; a follower needs it only where Settings' **Require Power Bash perk** says so (off by default, `docs/PROFILES.md`), and without it a power bash is not offered in the menu and a rule for one reads as unavailable ("has not got the perk Settings requires for it"). The perk has no entries: the gate is the idle tree, where `PowerBash` (0E8452, `bashPowerStart`, under the right power attack branch) requires HasPerk Power Bash **and** GetIsID Player (read 2026-09-15), so the tree never offers it to an NPC, perk or not. Only four NPC records carry the perk anyway (the Ebony Warrior and three Creation Club Hands). An NPC's power bash sent straight to the graph is not gated by either -- two landed in play on 2026-09-09 -- and the race data gives it a chance of 0.33. The combat AI's own come from its melee chooser instead, a right attack `CombatAnimation` carrying the drawn attack's event (49170, read 2026-09-15), and that is how a follower's is made now: three requests, three power bashes, each charged the 55 (`docs/ATTACK.md`). Falmer have a `bashPowerStart` idle of their own under their left attack branch, with no perk. The three blows share the snapshot's `Blow` (possible, perk, stamina, reach), the evaluator's checks, and Execute.

## 7. Use power, eat food and ingredients (built 2026-09-04)

**A power is a spell record** (`Type = Power` or `LesserPower`, `EquipmentType = Voice`, no cost). Embrace of Shadows 00088821 is one: Self, fire-and-forget, invisibility and night eye for 180 s. The racial powers live on the race record's spell list (`TESRace::actorEffects`), which the spell walk now covers, so an Imperial follower shows Voice of the Emperor under Powers without a console grant. Vanilla NPCs never use theirs: the combat inventory lists logged in `docs/MAGIC.md` had no power entries.

**The UseMagic package does not fire a power. Measured 2026-09-04**, Voice of the Emperor on Marcurio: on every request the package was selected (`current package after evaluate: OURS`) and the AI never cast it. Five leases, five `deadline, never cast`, not one spell-fire or shout event in between, only his own `CastStop`. The likely reason is the equip type: the procedure casts from a hand, and a power's slot is the voice. Nothing about the record is missing (no asset, no effect); it is the wrong performer.

**So `Use power` is performed through a Shout slot. Works in play (2026-09-05): Marcurio shouts Voice of the Emperor and the bandit stops fighting.** Skyrim.esm carries `ImperialVoiceOfTheEmperor` (0E40CB), a Shout record whose first word's spell IS the power spell 0E40CA, referenced only by the guards' shout-reaction dialogue: the game's own way of treating that power as a shout, and the player's power animation is the shout animation. The `Shout` package procedure is the UseMagic twin that casts from the voice. The ESP has eight of each: `FT_ShoutSlot1..8` (0x819..0x820, template Shout, IgnoreCombat, `GetFactionRank(FT_CastNow) == 8..15`, Location NearSelf, Target Self, HoldWhenBlocked off, copied from `MQ206HeroDragonrendShout`), each pointing at its own wrapper `FT_PowerShout1..8` (0x811..0x818, one word, spell = the Fast Healing canary), each word `FT_PowerWord1..8` (0x809..0x810). The pool in `Packages.cpp` is one array of sixteen slots, rank = slot index; `RequestShout` takes a free slot from the upper eight, writes the power into `wrapper->variations[0].spell` (a plain pointer on the record, no canary probe), adds the wrapper to the follower's base shout list for the lease (`TESSpellList::SpellData::AddShout`; the Shout procedure only fires a shout the actor has), and arms it as a cast is armed. Release is the voice's fire event; the deadline (3 s) is the never-started fallback, and steps back once on `BeginCastVoice` so a shout that has begun is never cut off.

Four runs settled three things, each by one change per run:

1. **The spell in the word must be Type Voice.** With a Power-typed spell in the word the follower shouted (`BeginCastVoice`, `Voice_SpellFire_Event`, the wrapper current) and nothing landed, every time. Every vanilla word spell is Type Voice. So for the lease the power's record reads `kVoicePower`, in memory, restored on release and on a game load; `IsLeasedPower` keeps it listed under Powers meanwhile. The alternative, our own Voice spells in the ESP carrying the power's effects, would need the active-effect check taught which spell stood for which power; this needs nothing, and the window is the two seconds of the lease.
2. **An NPC shouts the highest filled word.** With all three words filled the engine shouted variation 2, the three-word animation, a second long; with word one alone, variation 0, 0.3 s. Bethesda's own NPC shouts (Greybeard, draugr) fill all three and steer strength by word three's spell, which is the same rule seen from the other side. So a power goes in word one alone.
3. **No perk is needed.** The player's `AllowShoutingPerk` (0F11A9, entry point ModShoutOk, conditioned on Dragon Rising stage 90) is the player's alone: Arngeir has no perks and the Ebony Warrior's 36 do not include it. A copy without the condition was granted for the lease in one run and made no difference; removed.

Voice of the Emperor's Pacify is a calm on people. It showed nothing on the cave bear and stopped a bandit, which is why `bat ftman` exists (`docs/TESTING.md`).

**The Shout action** (section 2) rides the same slot with the shout itself in the package's Shout input, no wrapper and no type change: the follower already has the shout, and its words are Voice spells. A shout's delivery, for the menu, is its first word's. Built 2026-09-05, not yet played. The per-actor voice recovery (`Actor::GetVoiceRecoveryTime`, the high process's `voiceRecoveryTime`, a countdown set from the word's recovery when a shout fires, NPCs included) is in the snapshot, and a Shout or Use power rule inside it reports Recovering and waits, spending no cooldown: the engine's own cooldown, not a fixed one of ours. The wrapper's word carries a one-second recovery, so a power is gated by the same number.

**A follower shouts as far as the player's words reach, and no further (2026-09-16).** A word of power is unlocked once, for everyone -- bit 16 of the word's own form flags (`docs/VERSIONS.md`) -- and point 2 above is the problem: the engine shouts the highest FILLED word, not the highest unlocked one, so a follower with Unrelenting Force in their list would shout all three words off a record whose later words the player has never bought. So `RequestShout` takes the words above the last unlocked one off the record for the lease and `Release` puts them back, as the power's spell type is flipped and restored around the same window; a shout with no word unlocked is refused earlier than that -- it is not in `Snapshot::spells.known`, no menu offers it, the Shouts tab greys it and its voice cell takes no click. Built 2026-09-16, not yet verified in play.

**Equip power is deliberately absent.** A pin is a promise the AI will use the thing, and the vanilla AI never reaches for a power. The Shouts tab's Equipped cell for powers and shouts stays read-only. A mod that has NPCs choose between powers would make it one more kind in the pin book.

**Charge with a soul gem** and **Apply a poison** are the Then menu's Charge and Poison entries, a group of their own between the casts and the equips.

**Charge** spends a filled soul gem into the enchanted weapon in hand that cannot pay for its next hit, the right hand before the left. Strongest is the largest gem carried that would not overfill it; when every gem would, the smallest, which overfills least; weakest is the smallest carried; then every spendable gem by name with its soul, "Common Soul Gem (Lesser)". A reusable gem (Azura's Star, the ReusableSoulGem keyword) is a candidate like any other and is emptied rather than removed, as the engine's own routine does: the soul it holds is ExtraSoul on its inventory entry, and that is what comes off. The rule reports "no weapon" without an enchanted weapon in hand, and "charged" and waits when none in hand needs a charge. Performing it is what the engine's own recharge routine does, read from the executable: the gem's soul value (the five `iSoulLevelValue` settings, 250 to 3000) through the Mod Soul Gem Recharge perk entry point, added to what is left and capped at the full charge, written to ExtraCharge on the worn copy; the weapon's ability refreshed -- which is the step that copies the record into the hand's live actor value; the gem removed, or a reusable one emptied; the `UIEnchantRecharge` sound. The weapon's sheet shows the charge as "89 / 100 (89%)".

**Where the live charge is.** A wielded enchanted item's charge is not on the item: the engine keeps it as an actor value, `RightItemCharge` or `LeftItemCharge` by hand, which the HUD meter and the container menu read, and writes it back to the item's charge record only on unequip. Measured 2026-09-08: a staff cast down showed no charge record until it changed hands, and then 491 appeared. So the scan reads the hand's actor value for a weapon in hand and the record for one in the bag. Built and played 2026-09-08.

**Apply a poison** is the cascade's other half: Strongest and Weakest by the poison's effect, as the potions have them, then every poison carried by name. The rule needs a weapon in hand that takes a poison (anything but a staff) and reports "no weapon" without one; it goes to the right hand's weapon if that is clean, else the left's, so two firings dress a dual-wielder's both hands, and with every weapon in hand poisoned it reports "poisoned" and waits, as a buff rule waits on its buff. The engine's own routine, read from the executable: the inventory menu poisons the RIGHT hand's weapon only, never the left, refuses a second poison with "The current weapon is already poisoned." rather than replacing it, excludes staffs, and gives one dose plus the Concentrated Poison perk's entry point. The `Weapon: Poison none / active` condition (`docs/CONDITIONS.md` 7) is the pair for it. Performing it adds one dose to the worn copy's extra list and takes one bottle from the bag: the engine's own `PoisonObject` writes to an entry's first list, which for a follower carrying two of the sword need not be the one in hand. The dose is as many hits as the engine would give: one, put through the Mod Poison Dose Count entry point, which is where Concentrated Poison keeps its Set 2, so the perk counts for a follower or the player as it does in the menu, and a mod that retunes it is followed (built 2026-09-17, not yet seen in play; `src/game/Actions.cpp` has what was read from the executable for it). The vial sound the menu plays (`ITMPoisonUse`, a UI sound) is played too; the engine has no character animation for poisoning, the player's own goes on inside the menu. The Inventory tab lists poisons under their own heading, and a poisoned weapon carries the poison glyph after its name with a Poison section (name, hits left, effects) on its sheet: a dose is hits, not seconds, and stays on a sheathed weapon. Verified in play 2026-09-08: a dagger left and a sword right both dosed by two firings.

**Strongest and weakest, by effect (2026-09-08).** Potion, Food, Ingredient and Poison each open on Strongest and Weakest, then a divider and every bottle of the kind by name. Strongest and Weakest are submenus of the EFFECTS the carried bottles have -- "Health", "Stamina", "Magicka" for the three restores (and the three damages, for a poison), every other effect by the name the game gives it -- so a follower with no Resist Fire potion is not offered one. The effects are read off the bottles: a potion's or a food's boons (an effect not flagged detrimental or hostile), a poison's banes, and for an ingredient its FIRST effect alone, which is what eating one gives. The known vanilla effects come in groups with a divider between -- the restores; the fortify health, stamina, magicka; the regenerations; the resistances; the weapon and armour skills; the magic skills; invisibility and cure poison; then the skills no fight uses (barter, lockpicking, pickpocket, speech, smithing, enchanting) with carry weight and waterbreathing -- and any effect not in the table (a mod's, a custom brew's) comes after them, alphabetically, as one group (`core/Effects.cpp`). A poison's groups: the damages; the ravages and lingering damages; the regen damages; the weaknesses; fear, frenzy, paralysis, slow; the venoms. Cure Disease and Resist Disease are left out everywhere: a follower catches no disease. An effect's strength is its magnitude, and among equal magnitudes its duration (a lingering poison's "1 point for 15 s" beats its "1 point for 10 s"); an effect with no magnitude (Invisibility, Paralysis) is ranked by duration. The rule stores the effect's NAME (`"effect": "Resist Fire"` on the wire), never a form, so one rule reads any mod's potion of the effect and a profile shares across load orders; the twelve fixed policies of before (`drink-strongest-health-potion` and the like) still read, as the four of today with the vanilla effect's name. The evaluator chooses the bottle and puts its form on the decision's step, so the game side consumes a chosen bottle and a named one the same way. A potion whose effect is already in force on the follower at least as strongly (a Fortify, a Resist -- a vanilla Restore is instant and never is) is not drunk, as a buff is not re-cast; a STRONGER one is, since only the strongest of a name is in force ("What stacks", below). The two policies for two effects are two actions on two cooldowns. Only an ingredient carrying the VendorItemFood keyword -- a charred skeever hide, an egg, snowberries, or any mod's -- is listed at all: eating another ingredient only reveals what it does at the alchemy table, and a follower has nothing to learn. Weakest is for the everyday case, the cheap potion first with the strong one kept; strongest for the emergency.

**Any: the rolled choices (2026-09-16).** Two more shapes beside Strongest and Weakest, for the player who empties a bag of odds and ends into a follower and wants them used rather than naming each. **Any** -- **Any buff** for anything drunk or eaten -- sits at the head of the Poison, Potion and Food menus (`apply-any`, `drink-any`, `eat-any-food` on the wire) and rolls a thing outright; **Any** at the head of each Strongest and Weakest list rolls the EFFECT and then takes the strongest or weakest of THAT one, which is what an empty `"effect"` carries on the wire. An ingredient has the effect roll alone and no outright one: the few that are food are a thin bag to roll in. Both are listed whether or not anything carried would answer them, so the rule can be written before the bottles are in the bag; with nothing (for a potion, food or ingredient: no buff this follower can use), the item is dimmed, still a choice, and its tooltip says so. The effect is rolled before the bottle because magnitudes do not compare across effects -- 9 points of Damage Health against 20 of Damage Stamina is not a question with an answer -- so "the strongest poison" can only mean "the strongest of one effect". The roll is uniform over the FORMS carried and not over the bottles: nine of one poison come up exactly as often as one of another. An empty `"effect"` is "any"; a MISSING one is still a malformed record and the action is still dropped with a warning.

**What "any" may take, and why a potion is narrower than a poison.** Every bane a poison carries is a choice -- they all go at the enemy, so "put something on the blade" needs no further judgement. Anything drunk or eaten is narrowed to the BUFFS, because a rule that says "take something at the start of the fight" must not reach for the health potion being kept for the emergency. A buff is read off the effect RECORD and not off its name, so a mod's own Fortify counts without being listed anywhere: the archetype is **PeakValueModifier** -- the engine's own word for a temporary modifier it takes back when the effect ends -- the effect is a boon (neither Detrimental nor Hostile), and the bottle gives it a duration. Held against every vanilla alchemy effect (2026-09-16): every Fortify, Resist and Regenerate answers yes; every Restore is a plain ValueModifier flagged No Duration and answers no; Cure Disease and Cure Poison have archetypes of their own; a Weakness or a Slow is a PeakValueModifier and is caught as harmful. Invisibility is an archetype of its own and is no buff here. Waterbreathing is NOT, though it reads like one: every vanilla Waterbreathing effect, `AlchWaterbreathing` (03AC2D) included, is a PeakValueModifier flagged NoMagnitude (checked against the records 2026-09-16), so it would answer yes unless said otherwise, and `Buffs` leaves it out by the actor value it writes rather than by its name, so a mod's own goes with it -- it does nothing for a follower, and an "any" that rolled it would spend the item for nothing. Only the enchantment's version is a plain archetype, and the duration test keeps that one out anyway. Muffle is a PeakValueModifier too (`MuffleFFSelf`, `MuffleFFActor`, on MovementNoiseMult) and is left IN, since it does at least do something for a follower: noted rather than guarded. And it must do something for the follower drinking it: a Fortify One-handed potion writes `OneHandedPowerModifier`, which only a hidden perk reads (`docs/RESEARCH.md` 6), so an actor without the perk drinks it for nothing and it is no buff for them (`EffectApplies`, the same answer the sheet gives). Two edges, stated rather than guarded: a record that forgets the detrimental flag on a bane reads as a buff (ccASVSSE001's Slow does), and the duration is the BOTTLE's, which is what keeps a constant-effect record out. `core/Snapshot.h`'s `PotionStock::ChoosableBy` is the one place that says which of an item's effects a rule may choose it by -- a poison's banes, anything else's boons -- and `WantedByAny` narrows that to what an "any" may take; `game/Sensors.cpp`'s `Buffs` is the one place that judges an effect. The scan hands over every effect an item has, the bane beside the boon, so that decision is made in core and tested there (2026-09-17): a drink with a price is chosen by its boon and never refused for its bane, and no rule can name the bane.

**What the vanilla foods show (2026-09-16, via houseCARL, all 109 foods of the four masters at their load-order winners, Survival Mode in the order).** By the record 17 have a buff: Elsweyr Fondue (Fortify Magicka 100 and Regenerate Magicka 25, 720 s), Beef Stew (Fortify Stamina 25, 720 s), the soups and stews, the crostatas and dumplings, Braided Bread, Soul Husk, and four drinks. The other 92 only restore at once, or nothing. Where food is not a potion:

- **One name, two things.** Survival Mode's `FoodRestoreHealthDuration` (1058A2), on the soups and stews, is named "Restore Health" but is a PeakValueModifier on the heal RATE, 1 or 2 for up to 720 s -- Skyrim.esm's version, which Update.esm had turned into a plain heal over time and Survival Mode puts back. An apple's "Restore Health" heals up to 16 at once. So a rolled effect is chosen among the BUFFS of its name, or "the strongest Restore Health" would be the apple; a named "Health" policy still means every food of the name.
- **A heal over time that is not a buff.** Vegetable Soup's twelve-minute Restore Stamina is a ValueModifier with a duration, not a PeakValueModifier, and answers no. Consistent with the potions, where a restore is the emergency; noted because on food it reads like a boon.
- **Drinks come with a price, and it is paid.** Colovian Brandy, Firebrand Wine, Matze and Argonian Bloodwine each carry a Detrimental regeneration damage beside their boon (Damage Stamina or Magicka Regeneration, 25 to 60 for up to two minutes). The scan hands over both effects (`ConsumableEffects`) and core chooses the item by its boon alone (`PotionStock::ChoosableBy`), so a drink with a price is still an "any" choice and the follower takes it, penalty and all. Deliberate (2026-09-17): what an "any" asks is whether an item carries a buff worth having, not whether it is free, and refusing the whole bottle over its regeneration damage would put every wine in the game beyond a rolled rule's reach. No Strongest or Weakest can name the price, a bane not being something a potion or a food is chosen by; but the snapshot carries it, marked harmful, so a condition could read it if one is ever wanted. The panel shows it too: an item's sheet lists every effect on the record, a detrimental one as a negative amount.
- **Missed.** Homecooked Meal's lasting +25 to all three regeneration rates comes through a perk its Script effect applies, not through an archetype this reads; whether a perk-applied ability works on a follower at all is unknown. Survival Mode's hunger effects are ValueModifiers on Mood whose condition is the player, and never reach a follower.

**Where the randomness lives.** One `std::uint32_t roll` on the Snapshot, drawn per follower per tick on the game side; every "any" indexes with it. Deliberately not an RNG inside the evaluator: `ChosenForm` is called more than once per evaluation -- once to ask whether the action has what it needs, once to fill the decision's step -- and an evaluator that rolled afresh each call would answer those two questions about two different bottles. As a snapshot value the engine stays a pure function of its input, and a test names the outcome by setting the roll instead of sampling for it.

**What stacks (2026-09-16).** Alchemy effects of one name do not add: "Alchemy effects stack with enchanting effects, but not with one another -- only the strongest alchemy effect will be active, except Weakness to Magic and Weakness to Poison" (UESP, Skyrim:Alchemy_Effects). Different effects stack freely. So every choice -- a named Strongest or Weakest, an "any" effect, an "any" bottle -- is made only among bottles that would BEAT what is already in force: an effect is outdone by one of its name in force at magnitude at least its own (`PotionStock::Outdone`). By NAME and not by record, as a decision (2026-09-17): a food's Fortify Magicka and a potion's are different records (`FoodFortifyMagicka` 1058A8, `AlchFortifyMagicka` 03EAF8) and are one effect here, so a weaker dose of a name is never taken while a stronger one of that name is up, whichever of food and potion each is -- whether the engine would have let the two add up is not what decides it. The roll therefore never lands on a bottle that would do nothing, and with a Fortify Health up a follower rolls among the rest. A stronger bottle over a weaker dose is a gain and is taken; Weakest means the weakest bottle that is still a gain, since a 25 drunk under a 30 already running is not cheaper, only wasted. Nothing carried that would gain, with something carried, reads EffectActive ("previous dose still active", "every buff carried is already up"), not NoResource: the check for an empty bag asks with nothing in force, the check for "already up" with what is. Magnitude alone, not duration -- a second dose of equal strength only lengthens what is up, which is not worth a bottle mid-fight. What counts as in force is ALCHEMY sources only (a potion, a poison, a food, an ingredient): a worn Fortify One-handed ring shares the potion's name and writes a different value, and counting it would keep a follower off a potion that would have stacked. Not modelled: the Weakness to Magic and Weakness to Poison exception, which would take their names, and a table of names is what the record route exists to avoid; the cost is a follower passing over a second Weakness it could have stacked.

**A poison is steered by its target, not withheld.** A poison's banes land on whoever is struck, so a poison is chosen against the effects in force on the follower's current target (`Snapshot::targetRunning`, one more walk of an effect list, only with a target) and not against the follower's own. But that is a guess: the dose goes on the blade now and lands when a blow does, perhaps after the Slow already on the target has worn off, perhaps on someone else. So it only STEERS -- toward a poison the target is not already under -- and with every poison carried already beaten on the target, one goes on regardless, chosen as with no target at all. A potion drunk for an effect already in force is certainly wasted and gets no such fallback. An instant poison (Damage Health) never lingers on the target, is never in force, and is never steered away from.

**Food and ingredients** are the two lists beside Potion at the top of the Then menu, with the same Strongest and Weakest by effect. Both go through the same `EquipObject` call as a potion; the game consumes the item through its normal path. The snapshot tags every carried consumable with its kind, and a named consume rule checks form AND kind, so a hand-edited profile cannot drink a cabbage. Unverified in play: whether an NPC gets a food's or an ingredient's effect (the player eating an ingredient learns its first effect; an NPC has nothing to learn, and what the engine does instead is not documented). The potion's 3 s settle is used for both until one of their own is measured.

The Then cascade under Self reads, the more active thing first and a divider between the groups: Potion, Food, Ingredient; Cast, Dual Cast, Scroll; Shout, Power; Charge, Poison; Weapon, Arrows, Armor, Spell -- no Equip, Consume or Weapon heading above them (2026-09-08). Weapon and Spell open on Right, Left, Both, each hand on Unequip (let go of that hand's pin and take off whatever is in the hand, pinned or not; the AI decides again, so the hand is not kept empty; Both does both) and then what fits it. Arrows opens on Unequip (the quiver emptied; Armor's takes off every piece worn), then Strongest and Weakest -- the hardest-hitting or the weakest arrows carried, chosen again each time the rule fires, so the pin follows the quiver as kinds run out -- then the named arrows (2026-09-12). Under Enemy and Attacker: Attack, Power Attack; Bash, Power Bash; Cast, Dual Cast, Scroll; Shout, Power, a divider between each group -- what the weapon does, then what blocks (2026-09-15). Under the player, an ally or a named follower: Cast, Dual Cast, Scroll; Shout, Power; under a corpse, Cast and Dual Cast alone. Dual Cast is drawn only when a spell the follower can dual cast suits the target. A heading with nothing under it -- no food carried, no spell that suits the target -- is not drawn, and the dividers follow what is.
