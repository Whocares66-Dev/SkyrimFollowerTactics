FollowerTactics Engine

How a follower's rules, casts and pins run: three threads, one clock, and a rule engine that never sees the game.

Three threads, one owner of the game

Everything that touches a Skyrim object happens on the game's main thread. The other two threads never do. The pacing thread only decides when; the render thread only reads copies and posts requests.

flowchart LR
  P["Pacing thread
every 500 ms"] -- "AddTask(Tick)" --> G
  subgraph G["Game thread"]
    T["Tick()"]
    V[("Views
one copy per follower")]
    S[("Pins, rules, switches")]

  R["Render thread
ImGui panel"] -- "ObserveFollowers()

  R -- "SetRules, RequestWear

A task runs once on the game thread. A task that re-adds itself hangs the game; the pacing thread is the only clock.

The panel reads a FollowerView: a snapshot of her stats, inventory, spells, combat style and the last verdict of every rule, rebuilt by the tick and copied by the panel each frame. Nothing the panel draws is a live pointer. When a click needs the game (a pin, a rule change), it is queued as a task and answered on the next tick's view, or immediately where the request republishes the view itself.

With the framework's FreezeTimeOnMenu on, the tick is held while the panel is open. Requests still run, since they are tasks, and republish the view they changed. Anything the engine defers to the actor's next update (an enchantment's effect, a spell leaving a hand through the Papyrus native) lands after the panel closes.

What one tick does

The tick is the single loop. Its order matters, and it is the order below.

sequenceDiagram
  participant T as Tick
  participant F as Each follower
  participant C as Core (no RE)
  participant E as Engine
  T->>T: republish views owed after a spell unequip
  T->>T: hold if the clock is stopped (menu, frozen panel)
  T->>E: collect teammates within range
  loop per follower
    alt in combat, tactics on, not bleeding out
      T->>E: BuildSnapshot (stats, potions, spells, enemy)
      T->>C: Evaluate(rules, snapshot, state)
      C-->>T: Decision + Trace (a verdict per rule)
      T->>E: Execute: drink, cast, equip
    else idle
      T->>E: read stats only
    end
    T->>T: publish the view (inventory, magic, sheet, pins marked)
  end
  T->>E: TickPackages: withdraw cast leases that have fired or timed out
  T->>E: EnforcePins: put back a pinned item taken off (hands stand down in combat)
  E->>T: (in a fight) score this option? -- zero if a pin keeps it from the AI
  T->>T: sync menu entries, drop views of anyone gone

Measured cost of the combat path: about 47 us per follower, two orders of magnitude under budget.

The rule engine never sees Skyrim

The one architectural rule of the project: no RE:: type crosses into src/core/. The core takes a Snapshot of plain numbers and returns a Decision. That is what makes it testable without a game, and it is where the evaluator, the vocabulary and, as of today, the pin planner live.

Rules

A rule set is an ordered list of IF subject predicate [value] THEN action [form]. Evaluation is first-match-wins: rules are tried in order and the first that fires is the decision for that tick. Every rule gets a verdict, which is what the Tactics tab's Status column shows; the ones that matter are these.

verdictmeaning
firedThe condition held and the action was performed this tick.
falseThe condition did not hold.
cooldownThe condition held, but the action, the rule or the potion is on cooldown. Cooldowns run on game time, so a menu does not age them.
no potion / no spellThe action names something she does not have.
no magicka, busy, no targetThe action could not be performed right now.
off, invalid, n/aDecided before the condition is looked at: disabled, or a subject-predicate pair the matrix forbids.

Actions

Drinking is the direct route: the potion is equipped through the actor's equip manager, the same way the game's own NPCsUsePotions does. Casting is not. An NPC cannot be told to cast; she can be given a package that makes casting what her AI does. So a cast is a lease.

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Free
  Free --> Armed : rule fires, faction rank set, UseMagic package chosen
  Armed --> Casting : AI starts the cast
  Casting --> Free : her spell fire event names our spell, rank cleared
  Armed --> Free : 2.5 s deadline, never started

A follower's own UseMagic package goes at the front of their package stack, gated by a condition the lease points at them. One set of packages per follower, one cast at a time.

Pins

A pin says "this stays in this hand", or "this stays on" for armour and ammunition. Pins are the only state a person authors here; everything else about them is derived. The derivation is the core planner, tested; the game layer keeps one adapter from a record to the planner's description and imposes the answers.

  PIN["Pin request

A pin keeps its promise by three means, each covering what the others cannot.

mechanismcoversverified
Prevent-removal flag on the itemThe engine's equip-best swap: it refuses to displace a locked item.Pinned robes kept iron armour off.
Answer the combat AI's scoringThe AI reaching for anything else in a fight. It scores every option in its own per-follower list each time it decides, and the scoring call is ours: zero for what a pin keeps from it.A pinned bow held at melee range against a sword the AI re-listed every few seconds.
Watchdog on the tickWhatever got past both, out of combat. Hands stand down in combat so the AI and the pin do not trade blows.The dagger flicker before it did.

What competes for a pinned hand, from the planner's tests: a one-hand-only spell competes with a pin on its hand; an either-hand spell, a both-hands spell, a one-handed weapon and a two-hander compete with a pin on any hand, because the AI puts an either-hand spell into the pinned hand as readily as the free one; a shield or torch competes with a pin on the left. With both hands pinned the AI is left with the pins and nothing else, by design. A spell above her skill is never on the AI's list and cannot be pinned, since a pin is a promise the AI would not keep; it can still be equipped, and a cast rule can still make her cast it.

Nothing of hers is changed by a pin beyond the item's flag. Her spell list and her bag are never edited; the AI's list is rebuilt by the engine for every fight and discarded after. A pin changed mid-fight sets the list's dirty flag, and the engine rebuilt within 4 ms of play resuming in the measured case.

What is not there yet

  • Persistence. Rules, pins and follower switches live in memory. Phase 2's JSON profiles and the SKSE co-save are the fix; the runtime combat-style copy a pin can make is deliberately never saved.
  • Group subjects. The snapshot carries one enemy and no allies, so "any enemy below 30%" cannot match yet.
  • Equipment as a rule action. When it lands, a rule's swap has to release the pin it displaces, and the watchdog must not undo a swap a rule made.
