# Blows: power attacks through a UseWeapon package, bashes from a block

**Status: built 2026-09-15, not yet run in play.** Power Attack goes through a UseWeapon package on the follower's stack for the length of a lease, the way casts go through UseMagic (`dev/MAGIC.md`); Bash and Power Bash raise the follower's block before the bash, the way the engine bashes. "What was built" is the design; the sections before it are the research it rests on, read from the running 1.6.1170 executable and the game's records on 2026-09-15. Until then all three blows were one animation event each, sent with `NotifyAnimationGraph` (`dev/ACTIONS.md` section 6).

**The short answer.** A UseWeapon package can make the follower swing and power attack, and it cannot make them bash: the procedure reads the actor's attack data and skips every entry flagged Bash Attack, and no input changes that. A bash is an attack made while blocking, so a bash is asked for from a raised block instead.

## Why

The follower's combat AI drives the same animation graph, so an event sent from our tick usually arrives while a swing, a block or a recovery is in progress, and is turned away. Measured 2026-09-09: 3 of 11 blows landed. Seen again 2026-09-14 in Nordic Souls: a Bash rule on Ghorbash failed seven times in 34 s, five "already mid-swing" and two "the weapon is not drawn", each a `rule.actionFailed` warning.

Each of those also spent the action's cooldown. The core stamps the cooldown when it decides, and the refusal is found only at dispatch: `Execute` checked the weapon was drawn and the attack state none just before it sent the event. `BlowAvailability` in the core sees combat, a weapon, the target, stamina and reach, and cannot see either of those.

A package asks the AI to make the attack instead of pushing an animation past it. The record's IgnoreCombat takes the combat AI's hands away, as it does for a cast, and the procedure (below) retries on every AI update until the graph takes the attack, which a tick-driven event cannot do. That the follower then lands more blows is expected, not measured.

## What the procedure does

Read 2026-09-15 from the running game with `tools/livedisasm.py`; the IDs are the AE ones of 1.6.1170. `BGSProcedureUseWeapon` (vtable 203696) executes through 29381, which goes to the full update, 29383, when the actor's process level is high or middle-high; what it does at middle-low and low was not read. A follower near the player is high.

- **Its inputs.** The engine's parameter table has nineteen: Location, Weapon, Target, AlwaysHit, RepeatFire, VolleyFire, CrouchReload, DoNoDamage, HoldWhenBlocked, VolleyWaitMin, VolleyWaitMax, VolleysPerBurst, VolleyShotsMin, VolleyShotsMax, AllowCombatStart, AimWithoutFiring, AlwaysPowerAttack, DoHeadtracking, BlockPercent. The `UseWeapon` template (01C338) labels them for the Creation Kit: RepeatFire is "Never End?", VolleysPerBurst "End after this many Barrages:", VolleyShotsMin and Max "Min/Max Attacks per Barrage", AlwaysPowerAttack "Always Power Attack?"; it does not expose BlockPercent. None of the nineteen is a bash, and the CK wiki's page names none.
- **Which template maps which.** A package's procedure node lists, per parameter, the input it reads. The `UseWeapon` template's maps AlwaysPowerAttack to input 35; `UseWeaponAlreadyHeld`'s (0F7F4A), the template of Karliah's combat override in Blindsighted, maps it to 255, none. A copy of Karliah's record cannot be told to power attack.
- **A melee blow is drawn from the actor's attack data.** The update walks the attack data map on the actor's base record (`TESActorBase`'s `BGSAttackDataForm`, at +0x130), which carries the race's entries (`dev/ACTIONS.md` 6). It skips an entry flagged Bash Attack or Charge Attack (`flags & 0xA`), one whose chance is 0 (the sprint attacks), and, with AlwaysPowerAttack on, one not flagged Power Attack. With no stamina at all it also skips an entry whose cost is not nothing (26429, which asks a perk entry point); with any stamina, cost is not checked. What is left is weighted by `Actor::GetAttackChance`: the entry's chance, times the combat style's Attack Incapacitated or Power Attack Incapacitated multiplier when the target is incapacitated (47328; the staggered flag is the first thing it tests, the rest not read), or Power Attack Blocking when a power attack meets a blocking target. One is drawn at random.
- **It is performed through the action system, not as a bare event.** A `TESActionData` from the actor, action `ActionRightAttack` (default object 49), or `ActionRightPowerAttack` (70) for a power-attack entry, carrying the entry's event as its animation event, handed to 41557. 41557 sits just before `TESActionData`'s constructor (41558 in CommonLibSSE) and is a thunk through a manager singleton: by that, the perform-action entry mods call `PerformAction`, not confirmed by name. A check comes first (47297): the target within the attack's strike angle of the actor's heading (`Actor::GetHeading`, vtable slot 0xA4), offset by the attack angle and scaled by a factor the caller passes. Distance plays no part; the player's version of the check works from the camera, which is where "ProjectileNode" comes in. If the action is turned away, the next entry is drawn at once, and the whole draw runs again on the next update.
- **No hand, no bash, no dual action.** The only actions it fetches are `ActionRightAttack` and `ActionRightPowerAttack`, and for a bow or crossbow `ActionRightAttack` to draw and `ActionRightRelease` (51) once drawn. Nothing in the draw looks at what is in the hands: the Dark Elf race (Jenassa's) lists `attackStartLeftHand`, `attackPowerStartInPlaceLeftHand`, the dual-wield, hand-to-hand and mounted entries at chance 1, they pass the filter, and they would go out under a right-hand action. What the graph does with those is not known; turning them away would just move the draw on.
- **It waits for its own swing.** After an action is taken it watches the actor's own attack state and counts the attack when the swing is over. It does not attack while pathing.
- **In a fight it does not turn the actor.** Out of combat, before attacking, it points the actor's movement controller at the target (37834, through 37799 and 41615). When the actor `IsInCombat` (vtable slot 0xE3, asked by 38563) it clears that turn target and the head-tracking target instead (37839; 39472, which calls `HighProcessData::ClearHeadtrackTarget`), and leaves facing to the combat controller.
- **BlockPercent does nothing visible.** When the target is at Hit or Follow Through and a roll falls under BlockPercent, it calls 37706 with true and holds for 1.2 s. 37706 in this build is `xor al, al; ret`.
- **It ends itself.** When the pause after a barrage has run out, RepeatFire is off and the barrage count has reached VolleysPerBurst, the procedure succeeds (29479 sets done and succeeded), and the UseWeapon node of both templates, `UseWeapon` and `UseWeaponAlreadyHeld`, is flagged Success Completes Package. With VolleysPerBurst 0, as most records have it, that is before any attack. It also ends without success (29480) on failure paths, one of them after an equip call fails; the others were not identified.
- **It equips.** The update calls `ActorEquipManager::EquipObject` and `UnequipObject` itself, in the part that acquires the Weapon input's kind; when, was not traced. Those calls meet the pins' equip detour (`src/game/Pins.cpp`).
- **Distance.** Its Travel sub-procedure goes to Use Weapon Location, which Bethesda's own combat override sets Near Package Start, radius 500, not at the target. It makes no range test of its own. 39519, once taken for one, aims: an aim point on the target, the turn (37834) and the facing check (47297). The attack it performs finds its victim within the attacker's reach (`dev/ACTIONS.md` 6), so a request from too far swings at nothing.

## Who uses it

Across the load order (2026-09-15), 113 packages are built on the UseWeapon templates, and nearly all are staged: guards and trainees at practice dummies, archery ranges, Helgen's guards shooting at Alduin, Sovngarde, the Civil War barricades, Hearthfire's children sparring. Three override combat (interrupt override Combat): Karliah's and Brynjolf's in Blindsighted and `DLC2KagrumezHoldPositionFight2`. The combat AI never runs the procedure: its melee attacks come from its behaviour tree's chooser (49170), which shares only `GetAttackChance` and the reach check with it. Forcing an attack in a real fight is the rare use, and what we rely on.

The Attack action is not this: it points the follower at an enemy and leaves the choice of blow, spell or bow to the AI. A UseWeapon package forces a weapon attack, which is wanted for Power Attack alone.

## How the engine bashes

Read 2026-09-15 the same way.

- **A bash is a right-hand attack made while blocking.** In the idle tree, `bashStart` (01B417) is a child of `NonMountedCombatRight`, the branch `ActionRightAttack` resolves through, beside `NormalAttack` (`attackStart`), under three conditions: GetWantBlocking 1, IsBlocking 1, attack state not Bash. Under `NonMountedCombatRightPower`, `PowerAttack` (0E8456, for the player not holding block with a weapon in the right hand) comes before `PowerBash` (0E8452, `bashPowerStart`), which is gated on HasPerk Power Bash **and** GetIsID Player: the tree gives a power bash to the player alone, and only while blocking. An NPC's comes from the combat AI's melee chooser instead (read 2026-09-15): 49170 constructs a `CombatAnimation` of `ActionRightAttack` (44436), copies the drawn attack's event into its `ActionOutput::animEvent` (+0x28), processes it (44440), and on success keeps the time and the attack's recovery in its candidate list; nothing sets the actor's attack data there. With the entry `bashPowerStart` (the race data offers it at 0.33), that is a power bash the tree never chose.
- **The combat AI does it in two moves.** Its Block behaviour raises the block with `CombatAnimation::Execute(actor, 3)` and lowers it with 4. The engine's table behind those numbers, read from the constructor (44436): 0 `ActionRightAttack`, 1 `ActionRightRelease`, 2 `ActionRightPowerAttack`, 3 `ActionLeftAttack`, 4 `ActionLeftRelease`, 5 and 6 right attack and release again, 7 `ActionRightInterrupt`, 8 `ActionSneak`, 9 `ActionActivate`; CommonLibSSE's names for them are right. Two functions beside the Bash node (47866, 47867) lower the block too. The Bash node (47861, through 47841) and the Attack node (49199, through 49178) call the same melee chooser, 49170, each with its own list of candidate attacks, and 49170 performs the drawn entry as a `CombatAnimation` of `ActionRightAttack` carrying the entry's event: the UseWeapon procedure's shape, through `CombatAnimation` instead of 41557. That the Bash node's list holds the bash-flagged entries is inferred from the names, not read. `CombatAnimation::Execute` (44446) needs no combat controller: it builds the action on the stack and processes it.

## What was built

**Power Attack** (`src/game/Packages.cpp`). Each follower's records gain a UseWeapon package beside the cast and shout ones, made when the tick first sees them. It is a copy of `EdorfinAttackTarget` (055D48): an instance of the `UseWeapon` template, whose trigger is Self and which has no conditions. The copy is set once:

- Use Weapon Location near self, radius 10000; Edorfin's is his editor location, and the procedure travels there before it attacks.
- Always Power Attack and Headtrack on; Do No Damage, Always Hit, Hold when Blocked (an ally in the line of attack holds it), Never End, Pause between Barrages, Allow Combat Start and Aim Only off.
- One barrage of one attack: End after this many Barrages 1, Min and Max Attacks per Barrage 1.
- Weapon Drawn and IgnoreCombat, as the cast records, with the follower turned toward the target while the lease holds ("Facing" below); the `GetIsReference` lease condition every record of ours has.

The Int, Bool and Location layouts are found at load on authored values, each against a second record: Edorfin's trigger radius 350 against the Sovngarde heroes' 1500 and their three attacks per barrage; the heroes' pause on against Edorfin's off; Edorfin's editor location of radius 32 against the heroes' alias location of radius 425 and Edorfin's near-self search location. Vilkas's training package (0F7952) confirms Target to Attack reads a specific reference as Mercer's Target does, and Mercer's Spell input, which names one spell, gives the type Weapon Type is set to. The copy's location must be its own before it is written, and its target is written and read back. A check that fails leaves Power Attack to the animation event; the casts are not affected.

A request aims Target to Attack at the rule's enemy, names the weapon in the right hand in Weapon Type, and leases the record as a cast is leased. Edorfin's Weapon Type is an object type, all wearable items. Run that way (the first run, 2026-09-15), the template's Find step took the first such item in Jenassa's bag, a Staff of Flames; the procedure tried to equip it over her pinned Iron Sword every frame, the pin refused it every frame, and no attack came before the deadline, twice. Named, Find finds the weapon already held. The procedure completes after its attack, but its condition still passes then and the AI would pick it again, so the lease ends as soon as the swing does: a power attack seen in the follower's attack data after the AI picked the package up, then the attack state back at none (`power attack made`). A power attack already running when the lease began is not taken for ours. Otherwise the lease ends when the AI drops the package, or at a deadline: 3 s to pick up and strike, 3 s more once the swing is seen.

The action's cooldown counts from the end of the lease (`MinimumCooldown`, `src/core/Rule.h`), as every request's does. Counted from the decision, in the second run (2026-09-15), the 1.5 s ran out inside a lease of about 2 s and the rule fired again on the tick after release, into the follower's recovery; two of twelve requests failed that way, at 0.15 s and 0.9 s after the swing before. A power attack's `rule.resolved` carries its cost and reach and the follower's stamina, distance and attack state at the request and at the end, so a miss says why.

**Facing.** The records first copied the cast records' package data, IgnoreCombat included. In the third run (2026-09-15) three of four requests in one fight made no attack, with stamina and reach to spare and stamina unchanged. The procedure's facing check and its hands-off turning in a fight explain that: IgnoreCombat suspends the combat controller, so nothing turned the follower, and a power attack came only when they already faced the bear. Karliah's package data was tried next, copied from her record (Weapon Drawn, interrupt override Combat, no IgnoreCombat), so that combat would go on facing the follower while the procedure attacked. In the fourth run the AI picked the record up on none of five requests: `PlayerFollowerCombatOverridePackage` stayed current. That package comes from the alias's combat override list (`PlayerFollowerCombatOverridePackageList`, on SFF's `SFF_PrimaryMirror` as on vanilla's follower alias), a list apart from the package array the record is put at the front of; without IgnoreCombat a package there is passed over in a fight, and with it, it beats the combat override. Karliah's own override is in such a list. Splicing ours into it reaches only the followers whose alias has one: Serana's `DLC1NPCMentalModel` aliases and her own record have none.

So the record is back on IgnoreCombat, and the follower is turned toward the target: on every fast tick while the lease holds and no swing of ours is under way, through the call the procedure itself makes out of combat (37834: the actor's movement controller given the target's position, a tolerance of `fCombatAngleTolerance` degrees and two factors of 1), cleared on release (37839). Both are 1.6.1170 addresses; off AE the turn is skipped. Not yet run. `headingAtRequest` and `headingAtEnd` on `rule.resolved` say which way the follower faced.

**Where an alias's override lists are.** Not on the alias. Its loader (`BGSRefAlias::Load`, 24013) files a `BGSOverridePackCollection` for it in a global map keyed by the alias, and by the time the data is loaded the form IDs it read are lists (measured; where they are resolved was not read). At load `CheckAliasOverrideLists` (`src/game/Packages.cpp`) finds vanilla's follower alias, `DialogueFollower`'s Follower, in the map, with `PlayerFollowerCombatOverridePackageList` as its combat override list (+0x20). Read first from the running game on 2026-09-15: 310 aliases filed, the follower alias's list there as a list form, the table's count agreeing with its occupied slots. The map's address is 1.6.1170's (`dev/VERSIONS.md`).

**The override list route** (2026-09-15, not yet run). With the turning measured working (four of four made while the target lived), the record is tried where the running package is instead. `FindStack` puts it at the front of the list that holds the follower's running package: an alias's package array first, then an override list of an alias they fill (through the map) or of their own record. In a fight that is the combat override list, shared by every follower of vanilla's and SFF's follower aliases and gated, as every record of ours, by `GetIsReference`. There the record takes Karliah's package data, so combat goes on and faces the follower, and `TurnToward` is not called. On an alias array it keeps IgnoreCombat and the turning, as before. It comes out of the list on release, and on a load, since a list is a form that outlives the load. Casts stay on the alias arrays. `rule.resolved` says where each record went (`placedIn`).

The procedure attacks with the right hand alone, so the record is used only when the right hand swings (a blade or two-hander, or a blade in each hand). A left-hand blade and the fists go by the animation event, as before.

**Bash and Power Bash** (`src/game/Blows.cpp`). A request is steps: the weapon drawn and no swing in progress; the block raised with `CombatAnimation::Execute(actor, 3)` unless it is up already; once `IsBlocking`, a bash is the right attack action at the target, `CombatAnimation::Execute(actor, target, 0)`, which the idle tree resolves into `bashStart`, and a power bash is the same action with `bashPowerStart` set as its event (`CombatAnimation::Create`, `animEvent`, `Execute`), as the combat AI's melee chooser makes one, each asked again on each step while it is refused; the bash attack state watched until it ends (`bash made`), or 1.5 s, with the first other attack state kept (`attackStateSeen`) for a swing the tree chose instead; the block lowered with 4 if the request raised it. A request not done 2 s after it was made is given up, and says at which step.

Until 2026-09-15 a bash was `bashStart` sent to the graph too. In play the graph took it, from a raised block, on eight of eleven requests, bash and power bash alike, and no bash attack state followed on any of them: the event skips the action that sets that state, so the bash was not made by any measure the engine keeps. A power bash was sent that way until the chooser was read (below). As the chooser's action it works: three requests, three power bashes (2026-09-15), each from a block raised that tick, `attackEvent` `bashPowerStart`, the bash state 0.54 to 0.63 s, and stamina down 55, 55 and 54 -- `fStaminaPowerBashBase`, so the engine charged for them. The requests after each were refused for stamina, as they should be.

The right attack action from the block, in the next two fights (2026-09-15): 10 of 23 bash requests made a bash. Sent on the tick of the request, with the block up or raised that tick and the hands free, 10 of 12 did; sent on the tick a wait ended, for the follower's own swing or for a block coming up, none of 8 did, the tree choosing an ordinary attack (attack state 1) or nothing. So a request that waited holds the bash until the hands have been free with the block up for 0.25 s, starting over if the follower swings again or drops the block, and one still settling at the deadline ends `deadline, never steady`. `waited`, `handsFreeS` and `steadyS` on `rule.resolved` say which requests settled and when.

**Hands** (`src/core/Blows`), the right asked first and then the left, as a poison goes on. A power attack: the right hand's blade or two-hander, both at once when the left holds a blade too; else the left's blade, whatever the right holds; else the fists, with both hands empty. A bash: the right hand's weapon with the left hand empty, else a shield or a torch in the left. With neither the rule is skipped: "nothing in hand that swings", "nothing that blocks to bash with".

**The fast tick** (`src/game/Tactics.cpp`): every 50 ms while a power attack's record is held or a bash is in flight, and not otherwise. At the half-second turn the AI would lower a raised block between two steps, and the procedure could start a second power attack before the lease went.

While either is in flight the follower's other rules wait, as they do for a cast. Both report through `rule.resolved` (`dev/EVENTS.md`), with the timings and refusals that say which step held a bash up.

## Open

- **Whether it lands more.** `rule.resolved`'s `made` against `not-made`, for all three, against 3 of 11 by event (2026-09-09).
- **A block raised from the tick.** Whether the left attack action raises a block on a follower whose AI is fighting, how long `IsBlocking` takes, and whether the AI lowers it before the bash; `blockUpS`, `blockRefusals` and the deadline reasons are logged for this.
- **A plain bash, measured as a power bash now is.** Two of the bashes made on 2026-09-15 held the bash state 0.09 s against the others' 0.34 to 0.54 s, before `bashS` and the stamina readings existed. A bash the engine made should hold it about half a second and cost `fStaminaBashBase` 35; a shorter one with nothing spent was cut off, and by what.
- **The override list.** Whether the AI picks the record from the front of the combat override list without IgnoreCombat, as it picks Karliah's, and whether combat then faces the follower toward Target to Attack: the combat controller faces its own target, which can be a different enemy from the rule's. `placedIn`, `pickedUp` and `headingAtEnd` say.
- **Turning.** Whether the movement controller turns a follower running an IgnoreCombat package in a fight at all, how fast, and whether the combat controller undoes it. If it does not, `TESObjectREFR::SetAngle` sets the heading outright, on every runtime, at the cost of a snap.
- **Reach at a large body.** Whether the engine's measure, now the evaluator's (`reachDistance`, 2026-09-15), lets a follower at a cave bear ask for a blow where the fixed margin refused. And how close the combat AI stands: a melee item's maximum range is a stored value (+0x30; what fills it was not read), its optimal range a setting times that.
- **Hands.** What a left-hand, dual-wield, hand-to-hand or mounted entry does under `ActionRightAttack`, for a follower with a blade in each hand.
- **Stamina.** Whether a blow taken this way is charged; the same question is open for the events.
- **Pins.** Answered in part by the first run: the procedure equips what its Find step found whenever that differs from the hand, and a pinned hand refuses it every frame. With the weapon named it should find the one held; whether a named weapon ever makes it equip, and whether Find accepts a named form at all (no vanilla UseWeapon record names one; Find procedures elsewhere do), is the next run.
- **Low process.** What 29381 does below middle-high.
